How the calculator works

  • Data. Oxford Economics Global Cities Index 2026: scores and ranks for 1,000 cities, used with Oxford Economics' written permission. Peers are the 845 cities with fewer than 4 million people in Oxford's own metro populations. With no levers moved, the page reproduces Oxford's 97th exactly.
  • Two levels. A fitted curve turns each metric's value into Oxford's 0 to 100 score. Those scores combine into the five pillars and the overall score using weights that reproduce Oxford's published ranks exactly.
  • Anchored to Oxford. Every other city keeps its actual 2026 scores. A lever changes only Jacksonville's score, by the amount the fitted curve predicts.
  • Post-hoc. The fitted curves were chosen after the pre-registered methods failed to reproduce Oxford's formula. They match a second method (nearest-city lookup) to within 5 places. Treat every rank as approximate.
  • Not on the levers. Governance is set for the whole country, and educational attainment and life expectancy are measured for Florida, so they stay fixed. So do natural disasters, economic stability and age profile.
  • "Top 50 typical" is the median 2026 value among the Top 50 cities under 4 million. Matching it is a big ask on some levers: on public safety it means a homicide rate about 90% lower.
  • Source: Oxford Economics Global Cities Index 2026, used with permission. The "cities to watch" line is from news coverage of the 2026 index.
